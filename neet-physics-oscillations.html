Oscillations for NEET: SHM Concepts That Show Up Every Year

October 16, 2026

Oscillations and Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) are among the most consistent topics in the NEET physics examination. Every year, the test features 2-4 questions directly from this chapter, and the concepts frequently overlap with waves, sound, and electromagnetic oscillations. Mastering SHM isn't just about scoring marks—it's about understanding the fundamental motion that governs everything from pendulums to LC circuits.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll break down the core SHM concepts that appear repeatedly in NEET, share proven problem-solving strategies, and highlight the exact equations and scenarios you must master before exam day.

1. Fundamentals of SHM and Key Equations (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14)

Simple Harmonic Motion is defined as periodic motion where the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement and acts opposite to the direction of motion. Mathematically:

F = -kx

This foundational equation leads to the defining characteristics of SHM:

  • Displacement: x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ)
  • Velocity: v(t) = -Aω sin(ωt + φ)
  • Acceleration: a(t) = -Aω² cos(ωt + φ) = -ω²x
  • Angular frequency: ω = √(k/m)
  • Time period: T = 2π/ω = 2π√(m/k)

The NEET exam frequently tests your ability to identify whether a given motion satisfies the condition a ∝ -x. Common scenarios include mass-spring systems, simple pendulums (for small angles), and floating bodies.

⚡ Key Tip: The Phase Angle

Always pay attention to initial conditions. If the particle starts from mean position moving in positive direction, φ = 0. If it starts from maximum displacement, φ = π/2. This phase angle often determines whether your answer matches the expected result.

2. Energy in SHM and Conservation Principles (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14)

Energy conservation is the backbone of SHM problem-solving in NEET. The total mechanical energy in SHM remains constant and is distributed between kinetic and potential energy.

  • Total Energy: E_total = (1/2)kA² = (1/2)mω²A²
  • Kinetic Energy: KE = (1/2)mv² = (1/2)mω²(A² - x²)
  • Potential Energy: PE = (1/2)kx² = (1/2)mω²x²

NEET frequently asks questions about:

  1. Finding velocity at a specific displacement
  2. Determining amplitude given initial conditions
  3. Calculating the ratio of KE to PE at particular positions
  4. Time taken to reach specific velocities or displacements

The energy approach often provides the quickest path to the answer compared to kinematic equations. When you see a NEET question about SHM velocity, immediately think: v = ω√(A² - x²).

3. Simple Pendulum and Real-World Applications (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14)

The simple pendulum is a favorite NEET topic because it combines SHM principles with practical scenarios. For small angles (typically θ < 15°), a pendulum undergoes SHM.

Key relationships for simple pendulum:

  • Time period: T = 2π√(L/g)
  • Angular frequency: ω = √(g/L)
  • Effective length: Always consider how the setup affects L (e.g., conical pendulum, pendulum in accelerating elevator)

NEET loves questions about:

  • Pendulum in elevators (effective g becomes g ± a)
  • Seconds pendulum (T = 2 seconds, L ≈ 1 meter)
  • Compound pendulum and moment of inertia calculations
  • Coupled pendulums (rarely asked but important for advanced NEET prep)

When you encounter a pendulum problem, always first clarify: Is the pivot point accelerating? Is the medium resisting? Are we dealing with small or large oscillations? These details determine which equation to use.

4. Damped and Forced Oscillations (NCERT Class 11, Chapter 14 + Extensions)

While pure SHM appears most frequently, NEET occasionally includes damped oscillations and resonance concepts, especially in physics papers with higher difficulty levels.

Damped Oscillations:

In the presence of friction or air resistance, the amplitude decreases exponentially: A(t) = A₀e^(-t/τ), where τ is the damping time constant.

Key concepts:

  • Underdamped motion: Oscillations occur with decreasing amplitude
  • Critically damped motion: Returns to equilibrium without oscillating
  • Overdamped motion: Slow return to equilibrium

Resonance:

When an external driving force has a frequency close to the natural frequency (ω₀) of the system, resonance occurs and amplitude reaches a maximum. NEET questions test conceptual understanding: Why do bridges collapse? Why does a wine glass break at certain frequencies?
