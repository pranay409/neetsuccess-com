Biodiversity and Conservation for NEET: Key Terms and Exam Patterns

October 11, 2026

Biodiversity and Conservation stands as one of the most scoring yet frequently misunderstood chapters in NEET Biology. Covering approximately 2-3 questions in the exam, this chapter integrates ecology, evolution, and conservation biology while introducing students to real-world environmental challenges. Understanding the key terminology, categorizing biodiversity levels, and recognizing conservation strategies is essential for acing this section. This guide breaks down the chapter into digestible concepts aligned with NCERT standards and proven exam patterns.

Chapter 1: Understanding Biodiversity – Definition and Types

Biodiversity, derived from "biological diversity," refers to the variety of life at all levels of biological organization, from genes within a population to the different ecosystems across the biosphere. The NCERT Class 12 Biology textbook emphasizes three hierarchical levels of biodiversity that NEET consistently tests:

Genetic Diversity

Genetic diversity represents the variation of alleles (different forms of genes) within a species or population. This is the foundation of evolution and adaptation. NEET questions often ask students to explain how genetic variation enables populations to survive environmental changes. A classic example is the industrial melanism in peppered moths—a direct result of genetic diversity allowing survival under different environmental pressures.

Species Diversity

Species diversity counts the number and relative abundance of different species in an ecosystem. NEET frequently tests the concept of alpha diversity (diversity within a habitat), beta diversity (diversity between habitats), and gamma diversity (total diversity across a region). Understanding these terms helps answer questions about richness (species count) and evenness (distribution of species).

Ecosystem Diversity

This refers to the variety of different ecosystems and habitats within a region. India's incredible ecosystem diversity—from tropical rainforests to deserts, from mangroves to alpine meadows—is a favorite NEET topic. Questions often compare India's biodiversity hotspots with global standards.

🎯 Key Exam Tip

Memorize that India is a megadiverse nation ranking 7th globally in species diversity with over 45,000 documented species and 15% of the world's biodiversity. Questions frequently ask why India deserves this status despite occupying only 2.4% of Earth's land area.

Chapter 2: Biodiversity Hotspots and Conservation Priority Areas

Biodiversity hotspots are regions with exceptionally high species richness and significant endemism (species found nowhere else) but facing severe habitat loss. The NCERT curriculum emphasizes that identifying hotspots helps prioritize conservation efforts, a concept NEET tests regularly.

Global Biodiversity Hotspots

Approximately 34 biodiversity hotspots have been identified globally. These regions contain about 75% of the world's threatened species despite covering only 1.4% of Earth's land. NEET often includes questions about specific hotspots:

  • Tropical Rainforests: Amazon Basin (highest species diversity), Congo Basin, and Southeast Asian rainforests house countless endemic species
  • Tropical Coral Reefs: Coral Triangle and the Great Barrier Reef support extraordinary marine biodiversity
  • Mediterranean Basin: High plant endemism with unique Mediterranean vegetation
  • Indo-Burma Region: Includes parts of India and is recognized as a global hotspot
  • Western Ghats: A critical hotspot within India itself, with over 5,000 endemic plant species

India's Biodiversity Hotspots

India contains two recognized biodiversity hotspots: the Western Ghats and the Indo-Burma region (Northeast India). The Western Ghats, spanning four states along India's west coast, is particularly emphasized in NCERT and NEET exams. Its unique geography creates distinct ecosystems supporting species like the Nilgiri tahr, Malabar flying squirrel, and countless endemic plant species.

Chapter 3: Threats to Biodiversity and Conservation Strategies

The NCERT textbook and NEET exams extensively cover anthropogenic threats to biodiversity. Understanding these threats and their corresponding conservation strategies is crucial for scoring well.

Major Threats to Biodiversity

Threat Category Description Example
Habitat Loss & Fragmentation Destruction and isolation of natural habitats due to human activities Deforestation in Amazon; wildlife corridors broken by urban sprawl
Overexploitation Unsustainable harvesting of wild species Poaching of tigers, over-fishing of marine species
Pollution Chemical, physical, and biological pollution affecting ecosystems Industrial effluents killing aquatic life; plastic in oceans
Invasive Species Non-native species outcompeting native species Water hyacinth in Indian lakes; Australian rabbit in Europe
Climate Change Rising temperatures and altered precipitation patterns Coral bleaching; disrupted migration patterns
