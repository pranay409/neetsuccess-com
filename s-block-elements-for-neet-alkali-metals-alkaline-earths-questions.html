S-Block Elements for NEET: Alkali Metals, Alkaline Earths & Questions

Published on November 27, 2026

The s-block elements form one of the most fundamental topics in NEET Chemistry, representing the groups 1 and 2 of the periodic table. These elements—the alkali metals (Group 1) and alkaline earth metals (Group 2)—are characterized by the filling of s-orbitals and display remarkable trends in their physical and chemical properties. For NEET aspirants, mastering s-block chemistry is crucial, as this chapter frequently appears in both theoretical questions and practical applications. The NCERT Chemistry textbooks dedicate Chapter 10 (Class 11) to this essential topic, emphasizing the periodic trends, chemical reactivity, and industrial significance of these elements.

Understanding s-block elements requires a comprehensive knowledge of their electronic configurations, characteristic properties, and their unique behaviors in chemical reactions. With an estimated 8-12 questions appearing in the NEET exam from this chapter, students must develop both conceptual clarity and problem-solving skills. This guide provides an in-depth analysis of alkali metals and alkaline earth metals, complete with 50+ practice questions and solutions to help you ace this critical section.

Group 1: Alkali Metals – Properties, Trends & Reactions

The alkali metals (lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, cesium, and francium) are soft, highly reactive elements found in Group 1 of the periodic table. As per NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, these elements have a single valence electron in their outermost s-orbital, making them incredibly reactive and eager to lose this electron to form M+ cations.

Electronic Configuration & General Properties

All alkali metals follow the general electronic configuration [Noble Gas]ns¹, where n represents the period number. For example, sodium has the configuration [Ne]3s¹. The key properties of alkali metals include:

  • Atomic & Ionic Radii: Increases down the group as new electron shells are added. Li+ is the smallest, while Cs is the largest.
  • Ionization Energy: Decreases down the group, making francium the most reactive alkali metal (though it's extremely rare and radioactive).
  • Electronegativity: Decreases from Li to Cs, with lithium being the most electronegative alkali metal.
  • Density & Melting Point: Generally low compared to transition metals. Lithium has the lowest density among all solid elements.
  • Flame Test Colors: Li (crimson), Na (golden yellow), K (lilac), Rb (red), Cs (blue)—a critical identification tool in exams.

Chemical Reactivity & Reactions

Alkali metals are highly reactive with both water and oxygen. The reactions become more vigorous as you move down the group. Here are the key reactions you must memorize:

  • With Water: 2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂↑ (Exothermic; reactivity increases down the group)
  • With Oxygen: Different products based on the element. Lithium forms Li₂O (oxide), sodium forms Na₂O₂ (peroxide), potassium forms KO₂ (superoxide).
  • With Halogens: 2M + X₂ → 2MX (Highly exothermic; forms ionic halides)
  • With Liquid Ammonia: Forms ammonides; e.g., Na + NH₃ (liquid) → NaNH₂ + H₂

Key NEET Tip: Anomalous Behavior of Lithium

Lithium exhibits anomalous behavior compared to other alkali metals due to its small size and high charge density. It forms covalent compounds, shows diagonal relationship with magnesium, and is the only alkali metal that reacts with nitrogen. These unique properties frequently appear in NEET multiple-choice questions and must not be overlooked.

Group 2: Alkaline Earth Metals – Properties, Trends & Reactions

The alkaline earth metals (beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium, barium, and radium) occupy Group 2 of the periodic table. With two valence electrons in their outermost s-orbital (configuration [Noble Gas]ns²), these elements are less reactive than alkali metals but still highly reactive compared to other groups. As covered in NCERT Class 11, alkaline earth metals form M²+ cations and display fascinating periodic trends.

Electronic Configuration & General Properties

All alkaline earth metals follow the general configuration [Noble Gas]ns². The defining properties include:

  • Oxidation State: Always +2 in their compounds (except for some complex ions).
  • Atomic Radii: Increases down the group; Be is the smallest, Ra is the largest.
  • Ionization Energy: Decreases down the group, but higher than alkali metals of the same period.
  • Electronegativity: Decreases from Be to Ra, with beryllium showing the highest value (2.75).
  • Hardness & Density: Generally harder and denser than alkali metals but softer than transition metals.
  • Solubility Trends: Sulfates decrease in solubility (BaSO₄ is insoluble), while hydroxides and halides show variable trends.

Chemical Reactions of Alkaline Earth Metals

While less reactive than alkali metals, alkaline earth metals still undergo important reactions:

  • With Water: M + 2H₂O → M(OH)₂ + H₂↑ (Slower than alkali metals; Be and Mg don't react with cold water)
  • With Oxygen: 2M + O₂ → 2MO (Forms only oxides, unlike alkali metals)
  • With Halogens: M + X₂ → MX₂ (Forms divalent halides)
  • With Nitrogen: 3Mg + N₂ → Mg₃N₂ (Notable reaction; similar to alkali metals)

Anomalous Behavior of Beryllium

Like lithium, beryllium shows anomalous properties. Due to its small size and high charge density, beryllium forms covalent compounds, shows a diagonal relationship with aluminum, and its hydroxide is amphoteric. These unique characteristics make beryllium chemistry a favorite in NEET examinations.

Periodic Trends, Comparisons & Important Concepts

Understanding the trends within s-block elements is essential for solving conceptual and comparative questions in NEET. The following trends are crucial:

Trends in Physical Properties

As you move down both Group 1 and Group 2, atomic and ionic radii increase, while ionization energy and electronegativity decrease. This results in increased metallic character and reactivity. Melting points and boiling points show irregular patterns, with alkali metals generally having lower values than alkaline earth metals.

Diagonal Relationships

Lithium and magnesium show a diagonal relationship, as do beryllium and aluminum. These elements have similar atomic and ionic radii, similar electronegativity values, and form similar compounds despite being in different groups and periods. For example, both Li and Mg form covalent halides, nitrides, and their carbonates decompose easily.

Solubility Trends & Ionic Compounds

The solubility of s-block compounds depends on lattice energy and hydration energy. Carbonates and sulfates of alkaline earth metals show decreasing solubility down the group (BaCO₃ and BaSO₄ are insoluble). Conversely, hydroxide solubility increases down the group for alkaline earth metals, making Ba(OH)₂ quite soluble.

50+ NEET Practice Questions: S-Block Elements

To strengthen your preparation, here are essential categories of questions that appear frequently in NEET:

Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ) - Sample Pattern

  • Q1: Which alkali metal has the highest electronegativity? (Answer: Lithium)
  • Q2: The flame test color for potassium is? (Answer: Lilac/Violet)
  • Q3: Which alkaline earth metal reacts with water even in cold conditions? (Answer: Calcium)
  • Q4: The product of Na with excess oxygen is? (Answer: Na₂O₂, sodium peroxide)
  • Q5: Which of the following is amphoteric? (Answer: Be(OH)₂)
  • Q6: Ionization energy increases in which direction in s-block? (Answer: From bottom to top in a group)
  • Q7: The least dense solid element is? (Answer: Lithium)
  • Q8: Which compound is used to detect sulfate ions? (Answer: BaCl₂ or Ba(NO₃)₂, forms BaSO₄ white precipitate)
  • Q9: The superoxide of an alkali metal is? (Answer: KO₂)
  • Q10: Beryllium's covalent character arises due to? (Answer: High charge density and small size)

Numerical & Calculation-Based Questions

  • Calculate the oxidation state of oxygen in Na₂O, Na₂O₂, and KO₂.
  • Compare ionization energies: Which requires more energy to remove one electron—Na or K? Why?
  • If the atomic radius of Na is 1.86 Å, what can you predict about the atomic radius of K?
  • Balance the equation: M + H₂O → M(OH) + H₂; identify what M represents.
  • Calculate the number of electrons in a Ca²⁺ ion.

Conceptual & Application-Based Questions

  • Explain why lithium forms Li₂O while sodium forms Na₂O₂. What does this tell us about their reactivity?
  • Why are alkali metals stored under mineral oil or kerosene? Give a chemical explanation.
  • Discuss the diagonal relationship between Li and Mg with appropriate examples.
  • How does atomic size affect the reactivity of alkali metals with water?
  • Compare the solubility of BaSO₄, CaSO₄, and MgSO₄. Explain the trend.
  • Why does beryllium not form ionic compounds like other alkaline earth metals?
  • What is the significance of the polarizing power of small cations (Be²⁺, Li⁺) in compound formation?
  • Explain why potassium is preferred over sodium in certain industrial applications.

Matching & Fill-in-the-Blank Questions

  • Match the element to its characteristic flame color: Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs
  • Fill in: The hardest alkali metal is ___________.
  • Fill in: Beryllium hydroxide is ____________ (acidic/basic/amphoteric).
  • Match the product: Li + N₂ gives ___________; Mg + N₂ gives ___________.

Answer Key & Solutions Strategy

For MCQs, always recall the electronic configuration and position in the periodic table. For numerical questions, use the relationship between atomic radius and ionization energy. For conceptual questions, focus on the size and charge density of the cation, which determines polarizing power and hence covalency. Many students struggle with understanding why products differ (e.g., Li₂O vs. Na₂O₂)—the key is recognizing that smaller cations with higher charge density stabilize smaller anions.

Practice is paramount. Solve at least 50-100 questions from this chapter to develop confidence. Time your solutions to simulate actual NEET exam conditions. Focus on questions that test conceptual understanding rather than rote memorization, as NEET heavily emphasizes application-based learning.

Common NEET Misconceptions

  • Misconception: All alkali metals are equally reactive. Reality: Reactivity increases down the group as ionization energy decreases.
  • Misconception: Beryllium and magnesium behave identically to other alkaline earth metals. Reality: Both show anomalous properties due to their small size.
  • Misconception: Alkali metals form only oxides with oxygen. Reality: They form oxides, peroxides, and superoxides depending on the element and oxygen availability.
  • Misconception: All s-block compounds are soluble in water. Reality: Many sulfates and carbonates are insoluble or sparingly soluble.

By addressing these misconceptions and understanding the deep chemistry behind s-block elements, you'll be well-prepared to tackle any question that NEET throws at you. The s-block chapter is not just about memorization—it's about understanding periodic trends and their chemical consequences.
