Body Fluids and Circulation for NEET: Blood, Lymph and the Cardiac Cycle

October 03, 2026

Body fluids and circulation represent one of the most critical chapters in the NEET Biology curriculum. This chapter bridges multiple physiological systems and is consistently tested with 3-5 questions in the actual exam. Understanding the composition of blood, the role of lymph, and the mechanics of the cardiac cycle is essential for securing full marks in this section. Let's delve into each component with NCERT-aligned precision and exam-focused insights.

Blood Composition and Functions (NCERT Chapter 18)

Blood is a specialized connective tissue that comprises plasma and formed elements (cells and cell fragments). According to NCERT guidelines, approximately 55% of blood volume is plasma, while 45% consists of formed elements including RBCs, WBCs, and platelets.

The plasma component contains dissolved proteins (albumin, globulins, and fibrinogen), glucose, ions, and hormones. RBCs or erythrocytes lack a nucleus in mammals and are responsible for oxygen transport through hemoglobin. WBCs or leukocytes include neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils—each with distinct immune functions. Platelets are fragments involved in blood clotting.

Exam Pattern: NEET frequently asks about:

  • The normal RBC count in males (4.5-5.5 million/mm³) and females (4-5 million/mm³)
  • WBC differential count and their specific roles
  • Blood group inheritance and transfusion compatibility
  • Hemoglobin structure and oxygen-carrying capacity

Blood groups ABO and Rh factor are determined by antigens on RBC surfaces. This inheritance pattern is often tested through multi-step problems in NEET. The universal donor (O negative) and universal recipient (AB positive) concepts are frequently asked.

🎯 Key Tip for NEET Success

Remember the total WBC count is 4,500-11,000/mm³ with the mnemonic "Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas" for neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils in decreasing order. This helps solve comparative questions quickly.

Lymphatic System and Lymph Circulation (NCERT Chapter 18)

Lymph is an extracellular fluid derived from the interstitial fluid that surrounds tissue cells. It flows through the lymphatic system, which includes lymph capillaries, lymph vessels, lymph nodes, and lymphoid organs such as the spleen, thymus, and tonsils.

The key distinction between blood and lymph is that lymph lacks RBCs and has fewer proteins. However, it contains WBCs (especially lymphocytes) making it crucial for immune function. The lymphatic system is a one-directional system that returns tissue fluid to the blood circulatory system.

Important features for NEET preparation:

  • Lymph flows from tissues toward the heart via lymph ducts
  • Valves in lymph vessels prevent backflow, similar to veins
  • The thoracic duct is the largest lymph vessel, draining 75% of body lymph
  • Lymph nodes filter lymph and produce lymphocytes
  • The spleen filters blood and produces antibodies

NEET questions often test the distinction between the circulatory and lymphatic systems, and the role of lymph in immune defense. Understanding why lymph nodes swell during infections is a frequently asked application-based question.

The Cardiac Cycle and Heart Function (NCERT Chapter 18)

The cardiac cycle refers to the complete sequence of events that occurs during one heartbeat, encompassing systole (contraction) and diastole (relaxation). The cycle is divided into atrial systole, ventricular systole, and joint diastole, with a total duration of 0.8 seconds in a resting adult heart.

During atrial systole, the atria contract, pushing blood into the ventricles. Ventricular systole follows, contracting the ventricles and forcing blood into the pulmonary artery and aorta. The atrioventricular valves (tricuspid and mitral) close during ventricular systole, producing the first heart sound (lub). The semilunar valves (aortic and pulmonary) close during diastole, producing the second heart sound (dub).

Critical exam topics include:

  • Phases of the cardiac cycle with pressure and volume changes
  • The cardiac output formula: CO = HR × SV (Heart Rate × Stroke Volume)
  • Normal resting cardiac output (5 L/min)
  • The role of the SAN (sinoatrial node) as the natural pacemaker
  • ECG interpretation: P wave (atrial depolarization), QRS complex (ventricular depolarization), and T wave (ventricular repolarization)

NEET often tests the electrocardiogram (ECG) pattern, and understanding the relationship between mechanical events and electrical activity is crucial. A single ECG question can involve graph interpretation, abnormality identification, and calculation of heart rate from the ECG strip.

Clinical Correlations and Exam Applications (NCERT Integrated)

The examination frequently bridges theoretical knowledge with clinical scenarios. Hypertension, myocardial infarction, arrhythmias, and blood disorders are commonly tested in the context of physiological concepts.

For instance, understanding how atherosclerosis affects coronary circulation or how anemia reduces oxygen-carrying capacity requires integration of blood composition and cardiac function. Questions about the effects of exercise on heart rate, stroke volume, and cardiac output test comprehension of physiological regulation.

Preparation strategy: Create concept maps linking blood composition → circulatory pathways → lymph function → heart mechanics. Practice numerical problems involving cardiac output, stroke volume, and pressure gradients across heart chambers. Solve past 5 years' NEET questions to identify recurring patterns.
