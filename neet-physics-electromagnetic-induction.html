Electromagnetic Induction for NEET: Faraday's Laws Made Simple

Published on October 17, 2026

Electromagnetic induction is one of the most critical chapters in NEET Physics, accounting for 2-3 questions every year. The concepts introduced by Faraday and Lenz form the foundation of modern electrical engineering and are essential for understanding motors, generators, and transformers. This comprehensive guide breaks down these concepts into digestible pieces, grounded in NCERT curriculum with a focus on exam patterns.

Understanding Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction (NCERT Chapter 6)

Electromagnetic induction describes the phenomenon where a changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an electromotive force (EMF). This is the core principle behind Faraday's First Law.

Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction states: The induced EMF in a circuit is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux through the circuit.

ε = -dΦ/dt

Where:

  • ε = Induced EMF (in Volts)
  • Φ = Magnetic flux (in Weber, Wb)
  • t = Time (in seconds)
  • The negative sign represents the direction of induced EMF (Lenz's Law)

The magnetic flux through a surface is given by:

Φ = B · A · cos(θ)

Where B is the magnetic field strength, A is the area of the loop, and θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal to the surface.

📌 Key Tip for NEET Success

When solving electromagnetic induction problems, always identify whether the change in flux is due to: (1) change in magnetic field B, (2) change in area A, or (3) change in angle θ. Most NEET questions test your ability to correctly identify which parameter is changing and apply Faraday's law accordingly.

Lenz's Law and the Direction of Induced Current (NCERT Chapter 6)

Lenz's Law complements Faraday's Law by determining the direction of the induced current. The negative sign in Faraday's equation mathematically represents Lenz's Law.

Lenz's Law states: The direction of the induced current is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it.

This means:

  • When magnetic flux through a loop increases, the induced current creates a magnetic field opposing this increase
  • When magnetic flux decreases, the induced current creates a magnetic field in the direction of the original field to oppose the decrease
  • The induced current never aids the change—it always opposes it

NEET frequently tests Lenz's Law through scenarios involving:

  • A magnet moving toward or away from a coil
  • A coil rotating in a magnetic field
  • Two coils with changing current in the primary coil (mutual inductance)

Motional EMF and Faraday's Law in Moving Conductors (NCERT Chapter 6)

When a conductor moves through a magnetic field, an EMF is induced due to the Lorentz force on charge carriers. This is called motional EMF and is another application of Faraday's principle.

For a conductor of length L moving with velocity V perpendicular to a magnetic field B:

ε = B · L · V

Exam Pattern Alert: NEET questions on motional EMF often involve a rod sliding on conducting rails in a magnetic field. Students must:

  • Calculate the induced EMF using ε = BLV
  • Determine the induced current: I = ε/R (where R is total circuit resistance)
  • Find the magnetic force on the conductor: F = BIL
  • Analyze the motion dynamics using Newton's second law

A classic NEET problem involves a conducting rod sliding down inclined rails in a vertical magnetic field. Students must balance gravitational force, magnetic force, and use energy conservation to find terminal velocity.

Self-Inductance, Mutual Inductance, and Transformers (NCERT Chapter 7)

When current in a coil changes, it induces an EMF in the same coil (self-inductance) or in a nearby coil (mutual inductance). These concepts are fundamental to transformer operation.

Self-Inductance:

ε = -L(dI/dt)

Where L is the self-inductance (in Henry, H). The self-inductance depends on the coil geometry and is given by L = μ₀N²A/l, where N is the number of turns, A is the cross-sectional area, and l is the length of the solenoid.

Mutual Inductance: When current in the primary coil changes, it induces an EMF in the secondary coil:

ε_secondary = -M(dI_primary/dt)

Where M is the mutual inductance between the coils.

Transformer Equation (Ideal Transformer):

V_primary/V_secondary = N_primary/N_secondary = I_secondary/I_primary

NEET exams test:

  • Power transmission scenarios (step-up and step-down transformers)
  • Efficiency
